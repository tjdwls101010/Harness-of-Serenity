# LITE — the best operating trajectory here, and the price knows

**TLDR:** The cleanest stage-4 in the cohort: revenue +90% across five quarters and operating margin from −15.4% to +21.8%. Two things stop it being Tier 1 — the upside leg is already at spot on two-year consensus, and the equity is being part-funded by shareholders (SBC 10.8% of revenue, real FCF −$281.9M, net debt +$2.09B). **Hold, stop adding, re-add under ~$439.**

## Structural position (Bottleneck)

Transceivers plus **in-house laser capacity**. The vertical integration is the thesis: the independent map's sharpest criticism of this layer is that "final modules can be multi-sourced and price-competed while qualified InP epitaxy and high-power laser reliability remain scarce" — and that criticism is precisely what integration into the laser layer answers. LITE is not only the module; it owns a piece of the hop below it.

That distinction is visible in the numbers. Compare AAOI, held in the same layer with the same demand driver: over the same five quarters AAOI's gross margin went nowhere (30.6 → 29.1) and its operating margin stayed negative, while LITE's went 28.8 → 44.2 and −15.4 → +21.8. Same buildout, one converting it, one not. That is what owning the scarcer hop looks like on an income statement.

## Added after the fact — a funding price floor I did not have when scoring

**CONFIRMED, 2026-03-02: NVIDIA invested $4B — $2B each into Lumentum and Coherent — each with a multi-billion purchase commitment plus future capacity-access rights.** The reported consequence is that non-NVDA EML lead times were pushed past 2027.

Three things follow, and they matter:
- **A soft valuation floor.** A sophisticated counterparty put ~3.4% of LITE's market cap at risk inside the last six months, with diligence, and has every incentive to defend it in normal tape.
- **Moat evidence on the strategic plane.** This is the largest entity in the AI buildout deciding it structurally *needs* this supplier's laser capacity enough to pay for access rather than just order from it.
- **It does not change the arithmetic.** The upside leg is still −3.9%. **"Hold, stop adding" stands** — but the business underneath that call is better than I scored it, and the $439 re-add level is more likely to hold than a level with no such floor under it.

Cross-check from the same source set: the laser layer is **CPO-branch-invariant** — CPO needs *more* external CW lasers, at higher power and reliability, not fewer. So the architecture fork that threatens module assemblers does not threaten LITE's laser position. That is the strongest single argument for owning the integrated name over a pure assembler.

## The dilution read — why this is Tier 2, not Tier 1

The dilution question is not "is there a raise," it is **structure**. What the pipeline shows: SBC at 10.8% of revenue and total annual dilution of 10.8%, with real FCF at −$281.9M. So roughly a tenth of the company is being transferred to employees annually while the business does not yet fund itself in cash.

That is not the serial-ATM kill signal — this is a real business with a 21.8% operating margin, and SBC-heavy compensation is normal in this industry. But it is a genuine drag on a *multi-year accumulate* program specifically: you are buying a shrinking claim each year, so the business has to compound faster than 10.8% just to hold your share flat.

**What I could not check:** whether there is a live at-market ATM, and the terms of the debt behind that $2.09B net debt position. EDGAR returned 403 all session. The gate is therefore `conditional:`, not cleared and not failed.
