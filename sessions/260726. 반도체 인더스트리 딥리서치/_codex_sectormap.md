# Independent semiconductor sector map for an AI-buildout investor

**As of:** 2026-07-26  
**Mandate:** US-listed common stock, ADR, or OTC line; foreign-only winners are mapped honestly and resolved through the stated access ladder.  
**Scope:** structural accumulation candidates for future drawdowns, not a buy-today list. Valuation and all current financial quantities are deliberately omitted for the orchestrator's deterministic pipeline.

## TLDR

**DEDUCED:** the useful map is not `GPU -> foundry -> HBM -> lithography`. That is the visible end of the chain. The deeper stack separates into three very different kinds of scarcity:

- **DEDUCED — hard physical / qualification scarcity:** EUV mask inspection and parts of the mask-material stack; qualified engineered wafers and compound-crystal growth; certain package dielectrics and low-expansion glass; laser epitaxy; a narrow set of purity-critical consumables. These are the places where adding money does not create an interchangeable supplier before the relevant demand window closes.
- **DEDUCED — capital-and-time scarcity:** leading-edge fab capacity, HBM, advanced packaging, OSAT, silicon-photonics foundry capacity, ATE, and much of the subfab. These can choke a ramp, but capacity, yield learning, and customer qualification can eventually relieve them.
- **DEDUCED — standards, design slots, or execution risk:** EDA/IP, retimers, CXL, active copper, CPO, power-delivery architectures, module assembly, cooling, and most passives. These may create excellent profit pools, but one platform decision can often move the economics.

**CONFIRMED:** the portfolio already covers SK Hynix at HBM, `TSEM` at specialty and silicon-photonics foundry, `ONTO` at package inspection/metrology, `LITE` at laser/optical components, and `AAOI` at optical devices/modules. `NBIS` is a demand-puller rather than semiconductor supply ownership. **DEDUCED:** the least duplicated areas are subfab components, probe/contact interfaces, merchant memory-interface silicon, advanced-package OSAT, yield analytics, and upstream compound/engineered materials.

**Independent call:** the best long-duration hunting ground is the layer *under* whichever headline bottleneck is attracting capital. `AI accelerator demand -> package and optical complexity rises -> more process steps and more yield loss opportunities -> qualified subfab, test-interface, inspection, and materials vendors earn recurring content.` That link is **DEDUCED**; every company/product link below is separately labeled.

## Evidence and labeling rules

- **CONFIRMED** means the product, process role, or named relationship is publicly disclosed by the company, a standards body, an official depositary, or an SEC filing. It does **not** mean the equity captures meaningful economics.
- **DEDUCED** means the causal or investability conclusion is my inference. A deduction is never presented as a customer fact.
- **NEEDS VERIFICATION** means the fact can change—customer qualification, program volume, merger status, OTC tradeability, ETF composition, or current corporate status—and must be rechecked before action.
- **Constraint type:** **PHYSICAL** means demand cannot route around the scarce attribute during the buildout window; **CAPITAL+TIME** means capacity and qualification can ultimately be funded; **RISK** means the node can delay or impair a system but is not itself scarce; **HYBRID** means near-term physical/qualification scarcity becomes fundable over a longer horizon.
- OTC lines are named only when an official company or depositary source was found. An unverified grey-market symbol is not treated as an investable resolution.

## Recursive bottleneck map

The important causal chains are:

- **CONFIRMED links:** accelerator demand -> HBM and multi-die packages -> known-good-die and interface-yield requirements -> probe cards, sockets, ATE, inspection, and burn-in.  
  **DEDUCED value capture:** `FORM / COHU / TER / ONTO / CAMT / AEHR` can win across memory and accelerator vendors, but no single test insertion is guaranteed.
- **CONFIRMED links:** optics demand -> InP lasers/photodiodes -> qualified compound substrate and epitaxy -> MOCVD/ion-beam/wet-process tools.  
  **DEDUCED value capture:** `LITE / COHR / MTSI / AAOI` are the visible device/module layer; `AXTI` and `VECO` sit at the harder-to-expand lower hop.
- **CONFIRMED links:** advanced packages -> IC substrates -> ABF dielectric plus low-expansion glass/CCL/copper -> substrate yield and warpage control.  
  **DEDUCED value capture:** Ajinomoto and Nittobo can own scarce formulations while the US-listed OSAT captures none of that input pricing; `AMKR` is not a substitute.
- **CONFIRMED links:** more process and package steps -> more vacuum/plasma/fluid-control requirements and chamber contamination -> qualified subfab components and services.  
  **DEDUCED value capture:** `MKSI / AEIS / ICHR / UCTT` are the broadest under-owned “complexity tax,” but their OEM/customer concentration must be checked in filings.
- **CONFIRMED links:** CPO/optical engines -> silicon-photonics wafers/foundry plus laser/fiber attach -> package inspection and optical test.  
  **DEDUCED value capture:** the architecture can reallocate value between `SLOIY`, `TSEM/GFS`, laser vendors, connector specialists, and `ONTO/FORM`; there is no single CPO ticker.

**Second-order allocation effect — DEDUCED:** hyperscalers and accelerator/platform vendors will reserve capacity at the *qualified package, laser, substrate, and connector process*, not just at the final module assembler. The falsifiable signal is customer prepayment, co-investment, or capacity reservation disclosed by the upstream node. The current Amkor/NVIDIA arrangement is a **CONFIRMED** example at OSAT; equivalent evidence for most claimed optical/material bottlenecks **NEEDS VERIFICATION**.

## What a hype-anchored map gets wrong

1. **It calls all advanced packaging a physical bottleneck.**  
   **DEDUCED criticism:** interposer/RDL/OSAT capacity is usually a capital-and-time plus yield-learning constraint. The truly hard node may sit underneath in substrate, dielectric, low-expansion glass, temporary-bond chemistry, alignment, inspection, or known-good-die test.  
   **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong if funded qualified package lines come online yet output remains capped by an irreducible integration step for which no second process route qualifies.

2. **It treats “HBM shortage” as one indivisible moat.**  
   **DEDUCED criticism:** HBM scarcity decomposes into DRAM wafer starts, stack yield, bonding, packaging, thermal behavior, probe, final test, and customer qualification. The memory producer may not own the tightest step, and the tightest step can move between generations.  
   **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong if vendor disclosures show wafer capacity alone remains the binding limit after downstream bonding/test capacity and yields normalize.

3. **It promotes every silicon-photonics fab into a chokepoint.**  
   **DEDUCED criticism:** `GFS` and `TSEM` own valuable process platforms, but a customer-owned PIC can move after redesign/qualification, and unused capacity captures nothing. The foundry is a capital-and-time node unless a specific process has no alternative.  
   **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong if a volume customer publicly states that a proprietary foundry module is sole-source and cannot be ported within the product window.

4. **It mistakes transceiver assembly for ownership of the laser bottleneck.**  
   **DEDUCED criticism:** final modules can be multi-sourced and price-competed while qualified InP crystal growth, epitaxy, high-power laser reliability, or precision fiber attach remains scarce. `FN` is an execution vehicle; it does not own the optical physics.  
   **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong if module vendors disclose durable sole-source architectures and retain pricing despite broad component availability.

5. **It calls CPO a present bottleneck before CPO is a settled production standard.**  
   **DEDUCED criticism:** pluggables, active copper, near-package optics, and several CPO approaches can coexist. A demo, standards announcement, or ecosystem slide does not establish a volume insertion.  
   **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong when named production platforms disclose CPO as the default architecture and component suppliers show repeat volume qualifications across more than one customer.

6. **It calls retimers, CXL controllers, and active copper “physical bottlenecks.”**  
   **DEDUCED criticism:** `CRDO`, `ALAB`, `RMBS`, and `MRVL` can own excellent design slots, but a hyperscaler or accelerator vendor can remove or replace the part in a platform decision. This is a standard/IP profit pool.  
   **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong if a part becomes required by the standard with no interoperable second-source implementation.

7. **It uses `PLAB` as shorthand for the entire EUV mask chain.**  
   **DEDUCED criticism:** merchant mask production, blank material, pellicle, mask writer, and actinic inspection are separate layers. The deepest scarcity may accrue to `LSRCY`, Shin-Etsu/HOYA, or a private/foreign tool supplier while `PLAB` captures none of it.  
   **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong if `PLAB` publicly discloses ownership and material economics in the exact EUV blank/actinic inspection step.

8. **It buys an OSAT to express ABF or IC-substrate scarcity.**  
   **DEDUCED criticism:** `AMKR` and `ASX` consume substrates and ABF; Ajinomoto owns ABF formulation, while Ibiden/Shinko/Unimicron-class companies fabricate substrates. Input scarcity can hurt an OSAT instead of enriching it.  
   **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong if the OSAT contractually passes through the input increase and controls the scarce substrate allocation.

9. **It treats every “picks-and-shovels” conglomerate as having material AI exposure.**  
   **DEDUCED criticism:** `ENTG`, `MTRN`, `DD`, `PH`, `LIN`, `APD`, `VSH`, and `GLW` all touch relevant layers; the relevant business can still be too diluted to change the equity outcome.  
   **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong only when current segment/customer disclosures show the AI-linked product is material to consolidated results. That evidence **NEEDS VERIFICATION** through the pipeline/filings.

10. **It assumes more components automatically means more supplier pricing power.**  
    **DEDUCED criticism:** MLCC, inductors, connectors, and passives can see unit-content growth while multi-sourcing and annual cost-downs give the economics to the customer. Critical to function is not the same as scarce.  
    **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong if lead times remain structurally extended after capacity additions and suppliers disclose sustained allocation or contractual pricing power.

11. **It labels a US fab a winner merely because geography is scarce.**  
    **DEDUCED criticism:** jurisdiction is a real chokepoint only when the process is qualified, utilized, and economically required. A domestic shell without external customers is expensive optionality, not value capture.  
    **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong if customers put capital at risk through prepayment, above-market equity, or binding capacity reservations for the specific US process.

12. **It interprets `AXTI` geography as pure moat.**  
    **DEDUCED criticism:** AXT's China-linked crystal/raw-material footprint can create real scarcity and simultaneously make the equity hostage to export permits and customer dual-sourcing. Scarcity without reliable shipment is not monetizable.  
    **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong if permits remain durable and customers publicly retain AXT as a qualified primary source while competing non-China capacity fails qualification.

13. **It collapses probe cards, sockets, ATE, burn-in, and inspection into “test.”**  
    **DEDUCED criticism:** `FORM`, `COHU`, `TER`, `AEHR`, and `ONTO/CAMT` earn from different insertions, wear cycles, and defect modes. A customer can add one insertion while removing another.  
    **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong if a disclosed integrated test flow makes all five categories rise mechanically with the same unit volume.

14. **It assumes subfab suppliers are invisible monopolies.**  
    **DEDUCED criticism:** `MKSI`, `AEIS`, `ICHR`, and `UCTT` have sticky OEM qualifications, but OEM concentration, insourcing, and second sourcing remain powerful. Their merit is recurring complexity content, not guaranteed monopoly pricing.  
    **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong if an OEM publicly designates a subsystem sole-source across successive tool generations with no qualified alternative.

15. **It treats HVDC/GaN announcements as secured production.**  
    **DEDUCED criticism:** `POWI`, `NVTS`, `VICR`, and peers expose competing architectures. The rack voltage, isolation scheme, converter placement, and device material are still design choices. A reference board is not a shipment program.  
    **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong once named rack platforms disclose the supplier in repeat production and the architecture persists into the next platform.

16. **It calls cooling a semiconductor bottleneck.**  
    **DEDUCED criticism:** `VRT`, `MOD`, `NVT`, `APH`, and private/foreign peers solve an urgent system constraint, but chillers, CDUs, cold plates, and couplings are manufacturing and integration capacity. They are not lithography-like physical monopolies.  
    **Falsifiable test:** this criticism is wrong if one patented cooling interface becomes mandatory across major accelerator platforms without a licensed second source.

17. **It mistakes a strategic partnership for funded capacity without reading the terms.**  
    **DEDUCED criticism:** a press release may describe cooperation while the supplier funds the build through its own balance sheet or new shares. The Amkor/NVIDIA disclosure is unusually concrete, but exact risk-sharing terms still require filing review.  
    **Falsifiable test:** the “funded” label is earned only by evidence that the customer committed capital or binding take-or-pay economics; otherwise it is self-funded capacity.

18. **It ignores who can design the supplier out in one decision.**  
    **DEDUCED criticism:** module DSPs, retimers, NoC IP, PMIC topology, connectors, and cooling interfaces can all be displaced at a platform boundary. A physical substrate, qualified mask inspection method, or unique crystal-growth step usually cannot.  
    **Falsifiable test:** classify each node by the minimum action required to remove it. If a customer architecture memo can remove the part without rebuilding or requalifying the manufacturing process, it is not a physical chokepoint.
