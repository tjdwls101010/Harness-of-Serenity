## The propagation ladder — bottleneck order 1 → 11

| # | Layer | Why the bottleneck forms (Kiwoom) | Kiwoom's '26 status claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **GPU** | Generative-AI capex explosion; few suppliers can secure leading-edge process + HBM + packaging simultaneously; rack-level system supply complexity | GPU die shortage has EASED; the bottleneck has MOVED to system-level supply — HBM, packaging, power, networking |
| 2 | **Memory (HBM/DRAM)** | HBM stack count rising; HBM consumes more wafer per bit than commodity DRAM; server-DRAM priority allocation squeezes general memory too | Both HBM and server DRAM still in shortage; capacity expansion within '26 is limited |
| 3 | **CPU (server)** | AI inference / agents lift CPU demand; GPU orchestration and data processing; advanced process + packaging capacity allocated to GPU first | Lead times extended — Intel up to 6 months, AMD 8–10 weeks; **server CPU emerging as the new bottleneck** |
| 4 | **High-value packaging (CoWoS, EMIB)** | Large-area GPU + many HBM stacks must be connected; larger interposer = lower effective yield per wafer; 2.5D/3D packaging capacity grows slower than demand | CoWoS shortage persists (~10% shortfall estimated); **EMIB is the next bottleneck candidate** |
| 5 | **Substrate (FC-BGA, MLB)** | AI chip area and I/O count rising → high layer count, large area; longer process time and more panel area per unit; high-layer MLB productivity falls as process steps multiply | AI FC-BGA supply stays tight; high-layer MLB lead times extending, order backlog surging |
| 6 | **Upstream substrate materials (CCL, copper foil, glass fibre, drill bits)** | High-speed / low-loss requirements force high-spec materials; **T-glass** and **HVLP copper foil** have a restricted supplier set; fine drill-bit demand spiking | **T-glass: a structural bottleneck, tight into mid-'27**; HVLP copper foil prices rising and tight; shortage of high-spec drill bits |
| 7 | **MLCC** | GPU/CPU power density up → MLCC count and capacitance per board up; high-capacitance + high-reliability specs; limited qualified suppliers | High-spec MLCC for AI servers tight; some products seeing price hikes and longer lead times |
| 8 | **Power semiconductors (Power IC, MOSFET, SiC/GaN)** | Rack power moving from tens to hundreds of kW; **48V → 800V transition** raises part count and spec; high-spec demand surging | Commodity parts have slack; **high-efficiency AI-datacenter parts are tight**; major suppliers adding capacity |
| 9 | **Upstream equipment (packaging / substrate / inspection tools)** | Simultaneous capacity adds in CoWoS + FC-BGA + MLB spike tool demand; bonding/plating/inspection tools are high-precision with long lead times and long qualification; tool utilisation at its limit | Advanced packaging & inspection tool lead times extending; toolmakers' order backlogs at record levels |
| 10 | **CPO / optical comms (high-speed optical modules, silicon photonics)** — flagged by Kiwoom as an *additional expected expansion* | AI cluster scale-out drives bandwidth and power-efficiency demand; 800G → 1.6T transition; CPO integrates optical engine + packaging at high difficulty | CPO at the early mass-production stage; **1.6T optical modules, lasers and DSP upstream all tight** |
| 11 | **Semiconductor equipment (front-end + back-end)** — also flagged as *additional expected expansion* | Sub-3nm logic, HBM, commodity DRAM and eSSD all adding capacity at once; EUV / etch / deposition / inspection tools have high technical barriers and long build times | WFE market '25: **$115.7B (+11%)** (Kiwoom's figure, unverified by us); advanced-process, HBM, commodity-DRAM and eSSD tool lead times rising, backlogs expanding |

## How we use this document

1. **As a completeness check, not a thesis.** The ladder is a good scaffold for *where* to look — it is the sell-side consensus view of the bottleneck sequence, which by R1 means the layers it names loudly are the layers most likely already priced. Its real value to us is the ordering logic (bottleneck migrates down as each layer resolves), plus the two layers it names that consensus under-covers: **#6 upstream substrate materials** and **#10 CPO upstream**.
2. **Layers it OMITS** and our map must add: EDA/IP and ASIC design services · wafers and compound substrates (InP/GaAs/SiC/SOI) · photoresist, CMP, specialty gases and wet chemicals · photomask blanks and pellicles · probe cards, sockets and ATE · subfab components (RF generators, gas delivery, vacuum) · thermal at the die/package level (AlSiC, heat spreaders, TIM) · liquid cooling and CDU · optical connectors and fibre shuffles · memory interface chips (RCD/DB) and controllers · specialty/mature-node foundry (the layer that actually fabs silicon photonics and RF SOI) · the jurisdiction layer (US-located capacity as a scarce attribute).
3. **Claims to verify before use.** Every "current status" cell is a Kiwoom assertion. The ones load-bearing enough to check independently: the CoWoS ~10% shortfall, T-glass tight into mid-'27, server-CPU lead times, and the WFE market figure. Where our own sourced evidence disagrees, ours governs.
