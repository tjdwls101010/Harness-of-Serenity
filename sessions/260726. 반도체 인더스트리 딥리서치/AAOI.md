# AAOI — the ramp that isn't converting

**TLDR:** Revenue is genuinely ramping and the US-located laser fab is a real jurisdiction asset. But operating margin has been negative in all five quarters on file and gross margin is flat at ~30%, while LITE in the same layer went from −15.4% to +21.8% over the same window. Same demand, one converting it and one not. Don't add until gross margin moves.

## Structural position

Transceiver supplier with an in-house laser fab and US-located manufacturing. The jurisdiction attribute is the genuinely differentiated part — where security mandates or export controls push Western hyperscalers toward non-Chinese optical supply, a US optical fab is a scarce attribute a competitor cannot relocate into quickly. That is a real moat plane and it is why this is not simply a commodity module assembler.

The counter-argument, which the independent map makes and I think has force: module assembly can be multi-sourced and price-competed while the scarcity sits upstream in InP epitaxy and laser reliability. Vertical integration into lasers partially answers that — but "partially" is doing work, and the margin line is where you'd see it if the answer were complete.

## Valuation — lens run, both legs

`Lens (floor): 151.1 × 4 × (−0.086) = negative — no floor leg exists, operating margin is below zero`
`Lens (upside): 4.77 × 21.0 = $100.17` → +0% vs spot

(2-year EPS = $100.15 ÷ 21.0 = $4.77. At the PEG-fair multiple implied by its own 2-year growth, capped, the upside leg lands essentially at spot.)

Read: **there is no floor and the upside leg is at spot.** A name with no positive-earnings floor is valued entirely on the forward estimate, and that estimate requires a margin inflection that five quarters of data have not yet shown. The 0.26 PEG is arithmetically real and economically empty — it divides a forward multiple by a growth rate the margins aren't delivering.

## Gates

1. Monetization — **FAIL as of now.** Critical-to-the-chain but not converting to operating profit. This is the gate that caps the name.
2. Pricing realization — **partial.** Flat gross margin through a 51% revenue ramp is the definition of not realising pricing.
3. Survival — **pass, with a clock.** $440M cash against −$365.7M real FCF is roughly a year of runway at the current burn. Not distress; not comfortable either.
4. TAM expansion — **pass.** 800G→1.6T is a real content step.
5. Allocation control — **no.**
6. Demand breadth — **partial**, and unverifiable: customer concentration lives in the filings, and EDGAR was 403 all session.

## Cycle stage — 3, stalled at the margin line

The revenue ramp is stage-4 shaped. The margin line is stage-2 shaped. When those disagree, the margin line governs, because revenue without margin conversion is the signature of a price-taking assembler rather than a chokepoint owner.

## Priced-in

Priced in: the revenue ramp — the stock is up ~5.4× off its 52-week low even after a 57% drawdown from the high. Not priced in: nothing obvious to the upside; the market is already paying for a margin inflection that hasn't arrived.

## Downsides

- **The margin line.** Five quarters, no conversion. *Not priced in* — this is the thesis risk, not a detail.
- **Cash burn.** −$365.7M real FCF against $440M cash. *Not priced in.* With EDGAR down I could not read the financing structure, so I cannot tell you whether the next raise is contract-funded or an at-market ATM. **That gap is material and unresolved.**
- **Insider selling.** $119.1M sold, 42 sales, against $1.3M bought. *Not priced in.*
- **Volatility.** Beta 3.69, IV30 141.4, typical daily move 7.7%. *Priced in* — but it means position sizing, not conviction, is what protects you.

## Rating

**Tier 3 — hold, do not add.** The jurisdiction asset is real enough that I would not force a sale into this drawdown, but adding capital to a negative-margin ramp while a same-layer peer converts is the wrong use of the next dollar.

Vehicle: with IV30 at 141.4 on shares already owned, **covered calls** are the doctrine-correct instrument — harvest the extreme volatility while waiting for the margin line to move. Do not write CSPs to add here; that is adding.

Comparatively: within its own layer, LITE has already proven the conversion AAOI is still promising, and the two share the same demand driver. If you want more optical exposure, the honest answer is that you already have it — and that four of your six holdings converge on this one buildout.

**Falsifier (positive):** gross margin breaks above ~30% and operating margin crosses zero. That single print flips this to Tier 2 and makes it an add.
**Falsifier (negative):** an at-market ATM funding the burn, or gross margin below 28% on continued revenue growth.
