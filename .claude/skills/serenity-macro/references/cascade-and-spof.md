# Cascade patterns, cohort floors & hidden single-point-of-failure

Pull this when locating supply-chain read-throughs, judging *why* a peer sold off, mapping a cohort backstop, or stress-testing a "diversified" basket. All of it reads off the spine in SKILL.md Step 2: **Hyperscaler CapEx → neocloud → semis → memory → substrates/optics → raw materials.**

## Overflow pattern — and the tier-1-buys-on-open-market sold-out tell ⚠️ GOTCHA

When hyperscaler capacity is full, demand **overflows** to alternative providers (neoclouds, specialized compute) — sudden revenue acceleration the market hasn't priced. The confirmation that supply has *nowhere left to go*: **the tier-1 leader itself buying the scarce sub-component on the open market** = *structurally* sold out, not cyclically tight. Displaced second-tier buyers then scramble to the next merchant, re-rating the small name.

**The buy is the un-allocated #2/#3 underneath, NOT the sold-out leader.** The default reflex reads "leader sold out" as bullish for the leader — but the leader is already priced for it; the un-priced move is the displaced demand cascading to the next merchant down. And the leader buying on the *open market* (vs internal allocation) is the discriminator between cyclical tightness (resolves with capex) and structural shortage (physics-bound) — it's revealed preference louder than any order-book number.

> Leader buying its own scarce input on the open market → structural sell-out → the small #2 transceiver/substrate merchant re-rates.

## Sympathy selloff — split real counterparty damage from pure association ⚠️ GOTCHA

When one name reports badly, peers drop too. Run the mechanism check across the peers and separate:
- **Real counterparty damage** — a customer/supplier whose forward revenue actually takes the hit. May be real; re-rate.
- **Pure association** — sold off only for sharing a theme/sector, no revenue link. Mechanical, usually round-trips in **1–2 sessions**.

Both look identical on the tape (a peer down on a peer's bad print), but only one changes forward revenue — so the split is the forward-revenue test applied to a selloff. Getting it wrong means either buying a name whose real customer just vanished, or passing on a free 1–2-session round-trip.

## Strategic-incentive floor — map the cross-cohort backstop flow

When a hyperscaler's core demand is threatened by customer in-housing, it may **backstop alternative consumers** to defend its own revenue — an invisible price floor on the backstopped names. At the *macro* level, map these flows as a **cohort-level floor**: when a threatened larger entity defends a consumer tier, the whole tier's downside is propped, not just one name.

This floor is invisible to standalone analysis — it lives in the *backer's* incentive, not the name's balance sheet — so it changes the cohort's downside without showing up in any single name's metrics. Read as a *flow*, it tells you which whole tier carries a hidden floor in the current regime.

> A threatened hyperscaler backstopping its GPU-lender tier props the downside of every name in that tier — map the flow, then let analysis value the individual name's moat plane. ## Hidden single-point-of-failure — the diversified-looking list that is secretly one bet ⚠️ GOTCHA

A set of US-listed names that — **one hop down** — all lean on the same node (one region's supply chain: a single-island foundry cluster, one country's refining; or one driver: hyperscaler CapEx, one commodity, one policy line) is secretly **one bet** — fragile no matter how good each name is, and takeable-out by a single event however diversified the tickers look.

A list of different tickers *looks* diversified, but the diversification is an illusion if they converge on one node one hop down — a single event (an island earthquake, an export ban, a CapEx cut) takes the whole "diversified" book at once. **Surface the shared chokepoint when evaluating a theme; name it in the bear case.**

> A "diversified AI basket" where every name one hop down leans on a single-island foundry cluster — one quake is one bet, not many; name it in the bear case.

This is the *tail-risk* face of the same graph fact serenity-discovery reads as a convergence-find for the *alpha node* — keep both, they're opposite uses of one structure: the shared node is simultaneously the alpha (it *is* the bottleneck) and the tail risk.
