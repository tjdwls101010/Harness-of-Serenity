# Loss-hardened gotchas

These are the traps that cost real money — each one is a place the lens looks right and is wrong. They are the highest-signal lines in the engine: one of them prevents more wasted runs than a page of advice. Read them when a name's read hinges on a deduced link, a recent IPO/SPAC, a calendar dip, a reported number that seems off, a demo/prototype "win," or a category label.

### DEDUCED ≠ CONFIRMED
A supplier link deduced from materials physics FEELS like fact but can be the wrong vendor — the real one shows up, confirmed, at a trade show; an "unnamed leading customer" you pin to a specific buyer is often simply not that buyer. The reconstruction and transfer moves are powerful BECAUSE they infer links no filing states — which is exactly why they're wrong often enough to size losses. A DEDUCED node carrying a CONFIRMED node's conviction is the recurring miss. **Confirm via filing / press / conference before you size conviction on a deduced link.** Picture: a supplier deduced from materials physics that was the wrong vendor once the customer named its actual one; an "unnamed leading customer" pinned to a hyperscaler that simply never was.

### Limited-float round-trip
Within ~6-12mo of IPO/SPAC, price tracks TRADABLE float, not fundamentals — a thin tradable float lets a tiny amount of buying move price grotesquely, so a name can run 7x on ~1% of float actually trading, then collapse to the IPO price on unlock. The run-up was never fundamental and the unlock collapse isn't a buyable dip. **A post-unlock drop is MECHANICAL, not a fear-dip** — mistaking it for fear is the trap. Picture: a SPAC running 7x on ~1% float, then round-tripping to IPO price at unlock.

### Tax-harvest timing
A November dip in a down-YTD quality name is partly harvest selling that PERSISTS through November — the pressure is calendar-mechanical and keeps pressing, so buying the November dip means buying into continued forced selling. **Wait for December**, when it exhausts; don't read it as a clean fear-dip. Picture: a down-YTD quality name pressed through November by tax-loss harvesting, mean-reverting in January.

### Data-error mispricing
"Prove the math" assumes the reported numbers are real. A ticker-collision / stale / mis-tagged figure (a balance sheet showing -$82M cash when the truth is +$93M net) is ITSELF the mispricing — the gap between the artifact and the real filing is the entire trade, and trusting the feed blindly inverts a buy into a "broken balance sheet" pass. **Verify the actual filing.** Picture: DB 2009637599661510665 (VLN — ticker collision Valens vs Velan → phantom $82M inventory burn, mathematically impossible since $136.7M assets − $93.5M cash = $43.2M room; PT ~$493M base, +320%).

### Prototype ≠ production
A demo-stage qualification reads like a production win but routinely DIES at the mass-production cost-down — demo-locked designs get re-optimized out of the BOM, so a prototype qualification carries far more designed-out risk than its "design win" headline suggests. **Judge a supplier by where it sits INSIDE the customer's program** (prototype vs production-at-scale), and watch OEM/CES disclosures for a second source. The failure is inheriting production-win conviction from a prototype. (The `pre_commercial` flag is a related objective cue — but a real bottleneck at a cycle trough can show it too, so treat it as the prompt to run this gotcha and decide value-trap vs cycle-trough yourself.) Picture: a supplier qualified only into prototypes that dies when the customer re-optimizes the BOM at cost-down.

### Mis-classified character
You inherit a name's volatility/stage from its category label — but a "safe compounder" can move 17% in a day. A category label is a prior, not the name's actual stage; trusting the label over the name's own revenue/margin evidence is how you size a volatile name as if it were a compounder and get blindsided. **Read cycle stage from the name's OWN evidence, not the archetype you filed it under.** Picture: a "safe compounder" that moved 17% in a day because its true cycle stage didn't match its category label.
