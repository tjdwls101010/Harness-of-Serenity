# The 5-stage cycle table

Read cycle stage as an **evaluation** lens (how early & asymmetric, whether de-risking) — never as a sizing one (this engine does not size a book). Bind each stage to observable pipeline fields so the placement is reproducible rather than a vibe, and **re-read each quarter** — names migrate, and migration is what catches a name before the cliff. Magnitude peaks at stage 2; the thesis de-risks by stage 4 (the two don't peak together — that gap is the read; say which you hold).

| Stage | What it looks like | Pipeline tell | Asymmetry / return profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Guess the next bottleneck** | Not yet qualified; public info only; taxonomy, no revenue | no rev, no design-win | Highest magnitude, lowest probability |
| **2 — Qualified, no order ramp** | In the chain, no volume; design-win confirmed | rev_growth ~0, customer capex_flow up | **Highest returns, but LIVE designed-out risk** — magnitude peaks here, thesis unproven |
| **3 — Inflection, early** | Earnings + extreme projections; the easy point | rev_growth accelerating, beats begin, margins inflecting | Large |
| **4 — Inflection, mid-cycle** | Ramping, confirmed; "can't fight the earnings" | rev_growth high but YoY decelerating, consistent beats, margins expanding, RS strong | Smaller but obvious (~another 100%) — earnings-validated, magnitude mostly spent |
| **5 — End / structural** | Revenue cliff OR a structural compounder | rev_growth low/neg & margins rolling, OR a steady compounder | Low/negative — a cliff is an exit call; a compounder holds |

The stage sets how hard the verdict hedges and how loud the designed-out caveat must be. A capital-burning buildout is a 2/3; once spend converts to FCF it's a 4 — holding through the capex phase is the whole thesis. A stage-5 revenue cliff is an exit regardless of how good the story was.
