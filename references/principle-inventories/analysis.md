## SECTION 0 — Archetype Fork (step 0, the genuine fork)

### Name the archetype FIRST — it rotates discovery-question, gates, and valuation-anchor
- **What:** Before discovery or valuation, name the kind of opportunity: bottleneck (physical chokepoint demand can't route around) · disruption (a new entrant draining an incumbent's profit pool) · evolution (a step-change that just made a category investable). The discovery question, the winner-gates, AND the valuation anchor all rotate with it. Hardware/materials defaults to Bottleneck; relabel to Disruption/Evolution ONLY on positive evidence (a drained profit pool, a datable step-change) — never to unlock a softer lens.
- **Why:** Skip the fork and you walk a name down a funnel it doesn't have — forcing a payments disruptor or a launch-economics name through the physical-chain chokepoint funnel mis-frames it from the first move. The inverse error costs equally: reaching for a Disruption/Evolution story on a clean physical chokepoint just because its grade looks low under the floor manufactures a lens to justify the trade you wanted. The rotation is earned by the name's actual shape, not its grade.
- **Picture:** Semis are the worked Bottleneck instance throughout (a convenience of recent history, not doctrine). Legacy: "Force a payments or neocloud name through the chokepoint funnel and you mis-frame it from the first move."

### Bottleneck playbook
- **What:** *Discovery Q:* where does supply physically concentrate as demand scales? (chain-trace, recursive hop, confidential-link reconstruction). *Winner-gates:* monetization, pricing-realization, survival, allocation-control, designed-out. *Valuation anchor:* EV-multiple banding vs chain peers; when the input is tiny-TAM but gating, the enabler-material reframe.
- **Why:** A physical chokepoint with no alternative material/process is the only one of the three shapes whose pricing power is capital-proof — physics, not balance sheets, defends it — so its gates test whether that physical scarcity actually converts to dollars.
- **Picture:** DB 2004936335702753729 (AXTI/InP — Western AI roadmap tethered to ~$700M AXT + Sumitomo duopoly, demand ~2x supply, "hunger games" allocation).
- **Tags:** kind=principle | gotcha=no | model_already_knows=no | placement=skill_body
- **Source:** Archetype Playbooks / Bottleneck | correction=A2

### Disruption playbook
- **What:** *Discovery Q:* whose economics are being structurally attacked, and who captures the value draining out? (take-rate/interchange → cents; T+2 → instant settlement; closed rail → open). You're finding the toll-taker on a road being re-paved, not tracing a chain. *Gates:* (1) size of the incumbent profit pool under attack — larger and lazier is better; (2) the fee/take-rate delta — a 10x cost advantage, not 10%; (3) the moat captured as it wins (network effect, a standard, a regulatory license) — WITHOUT which the disruption is competed away. Gate (3) is the discriminator: organic, margin-durable share-capture with a real captured moat clears it; growth bought via acquisition or a subsidized take-rate that evaporates does NOT. *Valuation:* the market mis-anchors on the incumbent's category multiple; re-anchor on the disruptor's true driver — but earn floor-N/A by demonstration, then defend with a real number, never the label.
- **Why:** Most "disruptors" get competed away or are buying their growth; gate (3) is the only thing that separates a durable re-rate from a subsidy that runs out, which is why the bar is just as brutal as a chokepoint's.
- **Picture:** DB 1975205333254447126 (SNAP — "made biggest expense a revenue stream": monetize Memories storage + cut $190M GCP OPEX → ~$850M FCF → 35x EV/FCF → $31-33B MC). The soured-on-a-bought-growth-platform pattern is the gate-3 failure.
- **Tags:** kind=principle | gotcha=no | model_already_knows=no | placement=skill_body
- **Source:** Archetype Playbooks / Disruption | correction=A2

### Evolution playbook
- **What:** *Discovery Q:* what concretely just changed — a cost curve, a regulation, a technical threshold — that turned a long-promised category into a fundable one? Then: who controls the new standard? *Gates:* (1) what made it investable NOW — a datable step-change, not "it's the future"; (2) who owns the emerging standard / reference design the category converges on; (3) a strategic backstop. Gate (3) is BINARY and the discriminator: a contracted, customer-funded backlog (a Mag7 prepay, a strategic equity round above market) clears it; a buildout funded by its own at-market ATM / serial dilution does NOT — that name carries the de-risking story without the de-risking, and grades DOWN. *Valuation:* you're buying an option on category formation — anchor on the TAM the step-change unlocks and the name's claim on the standard, not trailing fundamentals; floor-N/A is earned by demonstration, then substitute a DEFENDED driver number.
- **Why:** Most step-change names are tourists riding the category's story; a backer plus a narrative is not a pass. The financing structure is the only thing that converts "promised build" into "de-risked build."
- **Picture:** DB 1969151544320016527 (NBIS — Mag7 customers + NVDA strategic incentive to prop GPU-lenders; $4.1B raised de-risks $17B MSFT capex; "$1M+ position, $225 PT").
- **Tags:** kind=principle | gotcha=no | model_already_knows=no | placement=skill_body
- **Source:** Archetype Playbooks / Evolution | correction=none

### Evolution gate-3: VERIFY which side — funded vs dilution (the one fact you can't take from the bull deck)
- **What:** When the whole BUY rests on the financing structure, read the FILINGS not the press release, and apply skepticism hardest to the name you like. The traps that flip "funded" to "dilution": **(a) a purchase / brand / "strategic" agreement is not funding** — a vendor letting you use its logo, or taking a risk-free convertible *in you*, puts zero third-party capital into the build. **(b) The tell is whether real capital is direct and contract-anchored** — a customer prepayment, an equity check above market, asset-backed debt secured on the offtake — versus the GPUs being bought off a **live, large at-market ATM sized near the market cap** (the dilution engine the "partnership" headline distracts from). A loud Mag7/NVDA "partnership" that reads closely as a logo deal funded by your own ATM FAILS the gate however bullish it sounds. Two neoclouds wearing the identical "we have a hyperscaler contract" headline can sit on opposite sides of this line — that line, not the headline, is the call.
- **Why:** "Funded" is the single load-bearing claim and the one most distorted by the bull narrative; a press release announces a relationship, a filing reveals who actually put capital at risk on what terms. Crediting an ATM-financed build as "funded" de-risks nothing — the issuer is betting with money it minted by selling the story to the buyers it dilutes.
- **Picture:** DB 1995084174223651180 (NBIS funded vs CRWV killed on $1.3B debt interest, IREN) — identical archetype, opposite sides of the line.

- **Source:** Archetype Playbooks / Evolution "Verify which side"; R4 | correction=none

### Tranche carve-out does not launder dilution
- **What:** "The GPU leg is funded, only the power-shell is diluted" holds ONLY if that funded leg is genuinely ring-fenced — bankruptcy-remote, non-recourse to the parent's at-market ATM. When the parent is still buying the build off a live, near-MC ATM, the whole name carries the overhang however you slice a press-release tranche, so it ranks BELOW the funded peer — and a narrative carve-out is NOT licensed to lift it back to a BUY. That override-up is the one place this read flips from a clean rank-below into a manufactured rubber-stamp; when tempted, run the kill #8 net-the-cash loop on the ATM cash ALREADY raised, not as a future trigger you defer.
- **Why:** A press-release tranche is a narrative partition; bankruptcy-remoteness is a legal one. Crediting the former as the latter is exactly how a diluting name gets rubber-stamped into the funded bucket — the precise failure the carve-out caveat exists to block.
- **Picture:** The two-neocloud split where one carves out "only the power-shell is diluted" but the parent's ATM is still live near MC.

- **Source:** Archetype Playbooks / Evolution; kill #8 | correction=none

### Evolution gate-3 INVERSE trap: a silent filing is not a funded confirmation
- **What:** A filing that is SILENT on financing is NOT a funded confirmation — the absence of a disclosed ATM is not evidence of contracted/asset-backed capital. When the financing structure isn't shown in the evidence, hold gate-3 at UNPROVEN and cap conviction there; lean "funded" only on a named, direct source you can point to (a customer prepayment, an above-market equity check, asset-backed debt on the offtake), never on a clean balance sheet or "no dilution disclosed" alone. A funded read carried only by the contrast with a visibly-diluting peer is BORROWED conviction, not earned — size it as unproven. (This is the analysis-side face of the absence-evidence hand-off: the pipeline can only flag the ABSENCE of a disclosed bad fact, never confirm the good one — so a silent field is a prompt to prove, never a pass.)
- **Why:** Both the funded and the dilution call require positive evidence; absence proves neither. Treating a silent filing as a pass lets a name inherit a funded peer's conviction without showing the contract — the same un-seenness that creates the lead makes you wrong (R3).
- **Picture:** The neocloud whose filing is silent on financing — held at unproven rather than waved through because the peer beside it is visibly diluting.

- **Source:** Archetype Playbooks / Evolution inverse trap; R3 | correction=none

### Span-two names: run both gate sets, weaker sets conviction
- **What:** Most names are one clean archetype. When a story spans two — a bottleneck INSIDE a disrupted category — run BOTH gate sets and let the WEAKER one set the conviction; where the two archetypes' valuation anchors disagree, the weaker gate set also picks which anchor governs.
- **Why:** A name is only as de-risked as its least-cleared door; letting the stronger story set conviction overweights the half that's working and hides the half that isn't.
- **Picture:** A chokepoint component inside a category being disrupted — gated on both the chokepoint gates and the disruption gates.

## SECTION 1 — Winner Gates & Moat Diagnostics

### Step 1 — bottleneck vs constraint vs risk: "can money solve this?"
- **What:** Classify the supply limitation — only one of three supports a multi-year thesis. **Bottleneck:** physical scarcity capital alone can't fix, concentrated, durable pricing power → multi-year conviction. **Constraint:** resolvable with enough capital + time, transient pricing power → tactical trade only. **Risk:** a probabilistic event, not a structural state → hedge, don't build. Boundary test: "Can money solve this?" If any amount of capital, given time, removes the scarcity, it's a constraint. *Physics trumps capital.* A true bottleneck meets all three: demand outstripping supply · oligopoly/monopoly · no substitute before demand peaks.
- **Why:** The distinction sets the holding horizon and the position type; mislabel a fundable constraint as a bottleneck and you marry a multi-year thesis to a pricing window that closes the moment capacity arrives.
- **Picture:** Legacy boundary test; "Physics trumps capital."
- **Tags:** kind=principle | gotcha=no | model_already_knows=no | placement=skill_body
- **Source:** Winner Gates / Step 1 | correction=none

### Step 2 — the 6 winner gates (a chokepoint is not yet a winner)
- **What:** A confirmed chokepoint is investable only if it clears all six; each doubles as a bear-case generator. (1) **Monetization** — does the position translate to revenue + FCF? Critical-to-the-chain ≠ money in the door; demand a modelable forward revenue ramp. (2) **Pricing realization (behavioral)** — having pricing power ≠ exercising it; ask not "can they raise price?" but "will they?" — and "will they?" is conditioned by JURISDICTION not only temperament, which lets you PREDICT non-realization on domicile alone (where law or culture vetoes aggressive hiking, or an externally-forced hike trips foreign-investment-screening law, a structural monopoly never converts). (3) **Survival to the ramp** — can the balance sheet last until monetization? Read the SEC context: contract-backed growth or value destruction? (4) **TAM expansion / value migration** — a static tiny-TAM chokepoint caps the return; winners expand downstream into the stack, then integrate upstream for margin. (5) **Allocation control = synthetic bottleneck** — control multi-year output ALLOCATION and you become the bottleneck even if someone upstream "makes" the input. (6) **Demand breadth / inelasticity** — selling to every player (yield/test/inspection) beats one customer on a dev contract.
- **Why:** "Not every bottleneck is a great investment" — the power-law bar is brutal because each gate is a distinct way a real chokepoint still returns nothing: a position that can't bill, won't hike, won't survive, can't grow, doesn't allocate, or serves one customer. Necessary, not sufficient. (Doctrine in reasoning ONLY — never spoken; reciting it is a voice forgery tell.)
- **Picture:** Gate-2: a sole-source feedstock maker that never raises price caps near book value — an 85% move, not the 5x a price-hiking equivalent delivers.
- **Tags:** kind=principle | gotcha=no | model_already_knows=no | placement=skill_body
- **Source:** Winner Gates / Step 2 | correction=none

### Pricing-realization is predictable from jurisdiction
- **What:** "Will they hike?" is gated by jurisdiction, not just corporate temperament — so you can predict non-realization BEFORE any name-specific evidence: where law or culture vetoes aggressive hiking, a structural monopoly never converts to realized pricing power, so discount its realization on domicile alone. Where an externally-forced hike would trip foreign-investment-screening law, the veto is LEGAL, not just cultural.
- **Why:** This converts gate-2 from a wait-and-see into a forward predictor — the corner-the-input-and-hike playbook works on US-exposed suppliers but stalls on allied ones whose norms cut against it, and that's knowable from the map alone.
- **Picture:** The corner-and-hike playbook that works on US-exposed suppliers but stalls on allied-nation suppliers (norms / FDI-screening veto).
- **Tags:** kind=principle | gotcha=no | model_already_knows=no | placement=skill_body
- **Source:** Winner Gates / Step 2 gate-2 | correction=none

### The 3-D moat graph: physical · financial · strategic · geographic
- **What:** Gates get evidence on four planes. **Physical** — BOM, bottleneck, criticality tier. **Financial** — debt/credit contagion travels DIFFERENT routes than product flow; two peers with identical customers can have opposite exposure by balance-sheet structure ("if the sector leader's credit cracks, does it reach this name through its debt?"). **Strategic** — who structurally NEEDS this company to succeed? A larger entity whose position depends on a smaller one will backstop it (an invisible floor); an active co-development partner transfers capability, dropping execution risk below standalone analysis. (This is the per-name version of the strategic-incentive floor — macro maps the same dependency as a cohort-level flow; the worked NBIS/NVDA case lives in the Evolution playbook.) **Geographic — moat or hostage?** The same concentration cuts both ways: a scarce node in one controlled region is a MOAT when the name controls and prices it, a HOSTAGE when the controlling STATE holds an export/permit lever over the name's own output. A US-listed maker that MANUFACTURES INSIDE the controlled region is often BOTH at once — net the two, don't read concentration one-directionally. (This net-the-two read is the single home for moat-vs-hostage; macro only carries the outside-beneficiary monopoly-premium catalyst half.)
- **Why:** Each plane is a route the others miss — a name can be physically irreplaceable yet a financial casualty of a peer's credit, or a geographic monopolist yet a permit hostage. Reading concentration one-directionally is how you mistake a hostage for a moat.
- **Picture:** A US-listed materials maker manufacturing inside the controlled region — beneficiary of scarcity AND trapped behind its government's permit wall (rare earths, gallium, neon, lithium processing generalize).
- **Tags:** kind=principle | gotcha=yes | model_already_knows=no | placement=skill_body
- **Source:** Winner Gates / 3-D graph | correction=none

### Comparative principle + inverse-proxy + PE-buyout signal
- **What:** Within a sector, the question is not "is this a bottleneck?" but "which is the BEST one?" — rank on integration depth (full-stack > capacity-only), margin quality, contract visibility, balance-sheet strength. **Inverse-proxy validation:** a well-funded competitor's FAILURE to replicate is the strongest evidence of moat depth ("who tried this and failed, and what does that reveal?"). **PE-buyout signal:** a financial-sponsor (PE) buyout of a tiny, obscure upstream node is independent, returns-driven validation that the node is a real chokepoint (distinct from synergy-motivated strategic M&A) — and since it removes the public entry, pivot the read-through to surviving listed comparables in that layer.
- **Why:** A failed well-funded replication attempt is harder evidence of a moat than any self-reported metric — it's a competitor's capital saying the moat held. A PE sponsor buys for returns alone, so its bet validates the node's economics without strategic-synergy contamination.
- **Picture:** DB 2027318568728273305 (IQE owns 100+ MOCVD reactors vs LandMark's 27-30 — the well-funded peer's inability to replicate the reactor fleet is the moat evidence).
- **Tags:** kind=principle | gotcha=no | model_already_knows=no | placement=skill_body
- **Source:** Winner Gates / Comparative principle | correction=none

### Architecture-identity check before accepting a commoditization claim
- **What:** When a name is dismissed as a commodity by comparing ONE headline spec (power, capacity, speed) against a bigger competitor, first verify the two implement the SAME architecture. If the smaller name's value lives in a distinct design that named lead customers chose for system-level reasons (thermal, power-per-bit, integration), the competitor "winning the spec in isolation" is a category error — they aren't substitutes. The customer's DESIGN-IN CHOICE, not the comparable datasheet number, reveals the moat; a confident single-spec commoditization claim is itself a tell the critic conflated architectures.
- **Why:** A datasheet comparison across different architectures is meaningless — the customer's system-level design-in is revealed preference that no single spec captures, and a bear who reduces a moat to one number has usually skipped the architecture check.
- **Picture:** A smaller compute/optics name dismissed on a single power/speed number while named lead customers chose its distinct design for thermal/integration reasons.
- **Tags:** kind=principle | gotcha=yes | model_already_knows=no | placement=skill_body
- **Source:** Winner Gates / Architecture-identity | correction=none

### Designed-out test = LIVE monitor (this IS kill #9)
- **What:** Designing a supplier IN is gradual; designing it OUT is one customer decision. Ask: does the position rest on PHYSICAL INEVITABILITY (no alternative material/process exists) or CURRENT CONVENIENCE (best option now, but alternatives could be built)? Physics-based = durable; convenience-based = fragile. But this is MONITORED, not answered once: watch leaks and OEM/CES disclosures that reveal a second source qualifying (a DISCLOSED qualified supplier is real evidence; conference HYPE is noise); while odds stay ambiguous, HEDGE and cut concentration rather than binary-exiting on the first rumor OR doubling down on hope; re-rate the floor only when the switch is confirmed. Peak danger has a shape — a prototype/dev-stage qualification is NOT a production-at-scale win (see the *prototype ≠ production* and *DEDUCED ≠ CONFIRMED* gotchas in SECTION 5 for the full mechanic): judge a supplier by where it sits INSIDE the customer's program.
- **Why:** Single-decision risk demands continuous monitoring, not a one-time verdict; binary-exiting on a rumor and doubling-down on hope are the two symmetric failures, and the prototype-vs-production distinction (SECTION 5) is where confident-looking design wins quietly evaporate at cost-down.
- **Picture:** A demo-stage qualification that reads like a production win but dies at the mass-production cost-down — judge the supplier by where it sits INSIDE the customer's program.
- **Tags:** kind=principle | gotcha=yes | model_already_knows=no | placement=skill_body
- **Source:** Winner Gates / designed-out monitor; kill #9 | correction=none

### pre_commercial = a hand-off, not a ruling
- **What:** The objective layer can raise `pre_commercial` (op-margin < -100%): operating losses EXCEED revenue, so no commercial business to value yet — a story priced on a promise. It is NOT a cap and NOT a verdict — just the objective cue to be skeptical, routed straight back to the gates. Override the skepticism ONLY on a moat you can show (a named design-win, a sole-source part, a gate the screen can't see), and first rule out a one-time charge (impairment, litigation, restructuring) dragging trailing op-margin below the line on a genuine business. Don't slam to AVOID either when a real revenue ramp is keeping it alive (a ramp holds it OFF avoid without earning a bull override UP). The same flag fires on a real bottleneck at a cycle TROUGH (capacity intact, margins about to inflect) — so it raises a Cycle fork (melting legacy vs cyclical trough) you resolve on revenue/margin TRAJECTORY and the designed-out monitor, never the snapshot. One behavioral resolver when trajectory is ambiguous: the TENOR OF CASH COMMITMENT — a supplier that can demand MULTI-YEAR PREPAYMENT to guarantee supply faces a structural shortage, not a cyclical one (revealed preference, louder than any order-book number).
- **Why:** The pipeline deliberately punts here because op-margin < -100% has at least three meanings (hype on a promise / one-time charge / cyclical trough) that only judgment can separate — treating the flag as a verdict would auto-reject a real bottleneck at the exact trough where the entry is best.
- **Picture:** DB 2016921538780680402 (CPSH — AlSiC pure-play, tiny-TAM defense material at ~$100M MC, pre-commercial but a DoD contract floor and a real AI-thermal ramp ahead).

## SECTION 2 — Valuation Doctrine

### The floor is EV/Rev + EV/FCF peer/chain MULTIPLE BANDING (corrected)
- **What:** The taught valuation lens is **EV/Revenue and EV/FCF multiple banding vs sector comps and chain-peers** — NOT a "no-growth x15" scalar. Present the floor (where it trades if growth stopped) before the upside; the GAP is the asymmetry, and a name near its floor with visible catalysts is the ideal setup. The pipeline surfaces raw EV/Rev and EV/FCF; the BANDING vs peers is agent judgment. The no-growth scalar may stay as ONE deterministic anchor among several, but it is not the lens. (Pre-revenue: floor inapplicable — say so, make the growth case primary, apply a larger uncertainty discount.)
- **Why:** "No-growth x15" is a doc invention he never said; banding a name's EV/Rev and EV/FCF against real chain-peers is how he actually argued floors, and a single scalar fabricates false precision a multiple band avoids by showing the comparable set.
- **Picture:** DB 2009637599661510665 (VLN — "fabless semi with 60%+ GM trade at 4x-8x EV/Revenue... $493.5M from $155m as conservative base"); DB 1975205333254447126 (SNAP "35x EV/FCF").
- **Tags:** kind=principle | gotcha=no | model_already_knows=no | placement=skill_body
- **Source:** Valuation Doctrine / dual valuation | correction=A2

### Forward P/E is PEG (judge the multiple relative to growth)
- **What:** Judge P/E RELATIVE to growth, not by its absolute level. The inversions that flip the naive read: a HIGH absolute P/E isn't a reject — **30-40x at 60%+ growth is CHEAP on PEG, and the biggest winners lived there**; a LOW P/E can be a trap — **12x at 5% is expensive**. <15x at 50% growth "screams" (PEG ~0.3 — paying a third of the growth rate, trajectory unpriced). Avoid = above sector comp at DECELERATING growth, regardless of narrative. Build a custom peer set for hyper-growth with no clean comp.
- **Why:** Judging the absolute multiple rejects exactly the names that compound (high-PEG-looking, low-PEG-real) and buys the value traps; under pressure the model reaches for the absolute number — the inversion is the edge that stops it.
- **Picture:** Legacy: "30-40x at 60%+ is cheap on PEG — the biggest winners lived there; 12x at 5% is expensive."
- **Tags:** kind=principle | gotcha=no | model_already_knows=yes | placement=skill_body
- **Source:** Valuation Doctrine / forward-PE gate | correction=none

### Value accretes toward the scarcest node (chain-multiple banding)
- **What:** Benchmark a name's multiple against the OTHER LAYERS of its own chain, not only its own growth. The irreplaceable input should out-multiple the assembler it enables; a SCARCER node priced BELOW the node depending on it is the mispricing — buy the scarce node. The inverse (a convenience node priced above its own irreplaceable input) is a kill/avoid flag. Set the correct ordering from a mature analog chain, then find a younger chain where the market inverted it.
- **Why:** A multiple is meaningless until divided by the denominator the structure demands; benchmarking only against the name's own history misses that scarcity itself ranks the chain, and the market routinely inverts that ranking in young chains because attention pools on the visible end-node.
- **Picture:** Sector-blind: a payment rail vs the app riding it; a battery cell vs the pack assembler.
- **Tags:** kind=principle | gotcha=no | model_already_knows=no | placement=skill_body
- **Source:** Valuation Doctrine / value-accretes; R5 | correction=none

### Bottom-up content × volume / MC sizing — the load-bearing DEDUCED move
- **What:** Size the scarce node bottom-up, from one unit of end demand: estimate the supplier's $ or % CONTENT per downstream unit (per robot, per ASIC, per TPU pod), multiply by the end-product's projected unit volume to back into "implied revenue from customer Y alone," then divide by TODAY'S market cap. **That ratio is the whole call** — the same BOM slice is already priced inside a $70B megacap but TRIPLES a $2.5B microcap's ARR, so you buy the small-MC leg with leverage to it, not the diversified incumbent. The content number is load-bearing and almost always confidential (triangulated from a CEO value-capture quote, a design-win disclosure, a chain map) — so tag it DEDUCED and size nothing on the multiply-out until a filing/transcript/conference confirms the per-unit figure (this is the DEDUCED-until-confirmed invariant — see CLAUDE.md); treat the ratio as a candidate-SIZER, not a pass (a fat ratio does not bypass the Winner Gates). **The trigger to run it is the moment you catch yourself reaching for the supplier's own PEG / P-S / trailing multiple and starting to write "priced" / "the easy money's been made."** On a design-win component maker feeding a COUNTABLE end-unit below a megacap, that top-down read IS the consensus lens — concluding from it is the error the gap is built on. Divide by the CURRENT pipeline-sourced `marketCap` (the never-eyeball-the-MC invariant lives in CLAUDE.md; it applies here because a wrong MC silently inverts the call). "Already re-rated / graduated out of the small-MC window" is legitimate ONLY when the bottom-up ratio on today's verified MC says so — never inferred from the dilution/concentration/ATM gates while the sizing math goes unrun. If you wrote the verdict without actually dividing content × end-volume by today's MC, the move did not run: the gates cap conviction, they do not author the priced-vs-cheap call.
- **Why:** This is the read nobody else runs (they model the supplier off its own historicals — which is why the gap exists), and it's the only move that turns a chokepoint-find into a price target. Skipping it and concluding "priced" from the supplier's own PEG re-derives the exact consensus mistake. But because the content number is unseen, the same un-seenness that mispriced it makes you wrong — hence DEDUCED until confirmed (R3).
- **Picture:** DB 2029219794025644382 (AAOI — at $7.1B MC, H2-2027 $4.35B ARR leapfrogs LITE's $55B FY26; "3x by next year"; content × hyperscaler-volume ÷ MC).
- **Tags:** kind=principle | gotcha=yes | model_already_knows=no | placement=skill_body
- **Source:** Valuation Doctrine / size-the-scarce-node; R5 | correction=none

### Framework-breaks: re-anchor, don't fake precision (the re-anchor cases)
- **What:** Strategic monopolies, policy-mandated demand, paradigm-shift growth, and disruptors all resist P/E and P/S. When conventional valuation returns an ABSURD result, SAY the framework is failing and re-anchor on the real driver. **Strategic monopoly** (a substrate maker priced at commodity multiples): anchor on subsidy scale, policy-mandated TAM floor, the strategic value of irreplaceability. **Disruptor** ("don't value it like its category"): name the legacy multiple the market is wrongly applying, then re-anchor on the disruption's economic driver — for money-movement, assets-on-platform × yield + take-rate capture, not a fintech P/E; for a margin-inversion, the OPEX line that just flipped to a revenue line. For a stablecoin/float-yield or pre-scale disruptor the no-growth floor is NOT applicable — say so and substitute the driver, don't print an "overvalued" verdict the floor fabricates.
- **Why:** An absurd conventional verdict is a CANDIDATE to re-examine the lens, never the conclusion — re-anchoring is earned by demonstration and a real checkable number, never the label. A wrong verdict means a wrong lens; it never licenses the trade you wanted.

## SECTION 3 — Cycle Stage & Timing

### The 5-stage cycle table (evaluation lens, not a sizing one; re-read each quarter)
- **What:** Read cycle stage as an EVALUATION lens (how early & asymmetric, whether de-risking) — never how much to hold (this engine does not size a book). The five stages: (1) **Guess the next bottleneck** — not yet qualified, public info only; taxonomy only, no rev; highest magnitude, lowest probability. (2) **Qualified, no order ramp** — in the chain, no volume; design-win confirmed, rev_growth ~0, customer capex_flow up; **highest returns, but live designed-out risk**. (3) **Inflection, early** — earnings + extreme projections, the easy point; rev_growth accelerating, beats begin, margins inflecting; large. (4) **Inflection, mid-cycle** — ramping, confirmed, "can't fight the earnings"; rev_growth high but YoY decelerating, consistent beats, margins expanding, RS strong; smaller but obvious (~another 100%). (5) **End / structural** — revenue cliff OR a structural compounder; rev_growth low/neg & margins rolling, or a steady compounder; low/negative.
- **Why:** The stage sets how hard the verdict hedges and how loud the designed-out caveat must be; binding each to pipeline fields keeps it reproducible rather than a vibe, and re-reading quarterly catches migration before the cliff.

### Stage migration is the hold thesis; equipment-first-then-pure-play
- **What:** A name migrates 2→3→4, and that migration IS why a thesis survives an ugly stretch (a capital-burning buildout is a 2/3; once spend converts to FCF it's a 4 — holding through the capex phase is the whole thesis). ### Dated sold-out + above-consensus contract-price hike = forward revenue at a fattening margin
- **What:** Watch for the discrete trigger most miss — a PULL-FORWARD of the qual/volume timeline vs prior consensus. Sharpest instance: a **dated sold-out horizon** ("sold out until 2027," "no relief until 2028") PLUS an announced **contract-price hike that exceeds the Street's modeled increase** is forward revenue at a FATTENING margin you can clock before the print — the above-consensus slice is near-100%-drop-through profit (a NAND +100% against a 33-38% model is ~65 unmodeled margin points). ### Falling-knife discrimination — the 4-step (run BEFORE responding)
- **What:** The pipeline raises a candidate via `absence_evidence_flags.no_fundamental_change_selloff → potential_entry` — it does NOT say "buy"; it flags "this drop has no disclosed fundamental cause, here's a candidate" and hands it to you to validate. (Absence-of-bad-news is all the code can see; it can never confirm the dip is safe — that is yours.) V1 says buy fear-driven dips, but V1 alone catches falling knives. When the flag fires (or any name you'd own drops hard), run all four before responding: (1) **Identify the mechanism** — mechanical/sentiment (MM hedging/option-pinning, algo misreading a one-time tax charge, margin-liquidation cascade, sector contagion by association, tax-loss harvesting, retail panic) OR a real fundamental change? Tell: mechanical drops reverse when the switch flips, and their magnitude is disconnected from real news. (2) **Prove the math** — does the scary headline actually hit the numbers? If it's BAD MATH (an energy spike denting <2% on a 60%-margin oligopoly; a "displacement" physically impossible mid-cycle), the gap between fundamentals and price IS the trade. (3) **Institutional-accumulation tell** — in a true fear-dip institutions accumulate INTO the drop (IO% rising, dark-pool prints) while retail panic-sells (13F lags, corroborate). (4) **Clear the kill signals** — if a real one fired (designed-out, dilution, sector-price crash, CapEx cancellation, restatement), it is NOT a fear-dip; step aside.
- **Why:** The whole edge of buying fear is that the drop is mechanical and the thesis intact — these four steps are the only thing that separates that from a real break, and skipping any one (especially the kill-signal clear) is how V1 catches a knife. Naming the triggering flag keeps the four steps from becoming orphaned: the pipeline's job ends at "candidate," yours begins at "validate."

### The 9 kill signals (full catalogue) + the principle that spots a 10th
- **What:** A kill signal isn't "the price dropped" — it's anything that BREAKS a load-bearing assumption of the thesis OR FLIPS the risk asymmetry so downside now structurally exceeds upside. Response falls out of which kind: a broken core assumption → the thesis is gone (exit, no trim); a mechanical, quantifiable, recoverable pressure → reduce/hedge and wait. (Acute case: **wrapper NAV-premium** — a vehicle holding illiquid private positions trading at many multiples of underlying NAV; the premium is itself the kill however good the holdings; with no float/options to short, the move is DON'T BUY, then expect violent reversion to NAV.) (2) **Suspicious fundamentals** — restatement, auditor change, rev-rec anomaly → exit immediately. (3) **Meme trap** — price fully decoupled from any thesis → trim to zero. (4) **Lockup expiration** — insider incentive to sell into the unlock → reduce/hedge, model the overhang. (5) **Inverse Cathie Wood** — ARKK accumulation as a hype-peak warning → tighten discipline, re-examine the bear case (not auto-exit). (6) **Sector-collapse signal** — the chain's leading indicator crashes (NAND/DRAM spot, fab utilization) → reassess the WHOLE chain. (7) **CapEx cancellation** — a downstream customer cancels/delays CapEx; if >20% of forward model, thesis may be broken. (8) **Dilution** — read the STRUCTURE (own item below). (9) **Designed-out** — own item below. Spot a NOVEL kill (#10) by the principle, not the list.

### Kill #8 — dilution: read the STRUCTURE before calling it a kill (both directions)
- **What:** The true kill is SERIAL, value-destroying ATM — management that habitually prints stock into the open market to fund the burn → exit on the PATTERN, not the first raise. A single raise is a capital-markets event whose structure decides everything: **instrument** (a premium-priced convertible >> an at-market ATM), **coupon** (0% >> 9%), **use-of-proceeds** (a named contract-backed build >> general runway), and whether it's ALREADY priced in. Contract-funded / 0%-coupon / pre-announced dilution is often a BUYABLE DIP. The structure-read runs BOTH directions: **price the raise against spot** — when a sophisticated counterparty (convertible, PIPE, strategic round) pays ABOVE market, that premium is a conviction floor above spot (the raise-minus-spot gap is a front-runnable bullish anomaly, inverse of the default "raises come at a discount"; this is the dilution-side face of the funding-price-floor item in SECTION 2). And **invert the buyback's friendly reputation** — a buyback funded by ISSUING DEBT and sized to roughly offset annual stock-comp is not a return of capital; it masks SBC dilution and quietly flips the balance sheet to net debt (reconstruct as-if-cash FCF to tell it from a real buyback paid out of surplus). One tell INSIDE this kill: the **circular self-justification loop** — management dilutes retail off a live ATM to hoard cash, then points to that cash pile to claim a higher "deserved" MC and awards itself SBC out of the proceeds; that is value RELOCATED from new buyers to insiders, not created, so a fat balance sheet BUILT FROM at-market issuance is NOT a floor. Net the cash against the shares it cost and the SBC it funds before crediting a dollar; cash raised off a live ATM is a red flag, not an asset.
- **Why:** The pipeline flags only the quant; the kill-vs-buyable call lives entirely in the 8-K/424B structure it can't read — and treating any dilution as a kill misses the contract-funded buyable dip, while treating any raise as benign misses the serial ATM. The net-the-cash discipline exists because an ATM-built balance sheet looks like strength and is actually value relocated to insiders.

### Kill #9 — designed-out: scope WHICH LAYER the cut lands on (the inversion)
- **What:** Designed-out (customer develops an alternative, a cheaper source emerges, a tech shift removes the need) responds per the Winner-Gates monitor (confirm → exit; ambiguous rumor → hedge/cut not binary-exit; watch hardest at the mass-production cost-down). ### Conviction dynamics: strengthen · erode · inherit · post-mortem

- **Why:** Conviction with no standing of its own — a continuous mark on (confirming evidence − the live falsifier) — is what prevents both averaging into a broken thesis and nursing a zombie; the post-mortem taxonomy turns each loss into a fixable category instead of a vague "I was wrong."

### DEDUCED ≠ CONFIRMED

- **Picture:** A supplier deduced from materials physics that was the wrong vendor once the customer named its actual one at a trade show; an "unnamed leading customer" pinned to a hyperscaler that simply never was.

### Limited-float round-trip
- **What:** Within ~6-12mo of IPO/SPAC, price tracks TRADABLE float, not fundamentals — a name can run 7x on ~1% of float actually trading, then collapse to the IPO price on unlock. A post-unlock drop is MECHANICAL, not a fear-dip.
